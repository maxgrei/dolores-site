# Team Dolores: 3-minute demo

A walkthrough for presenting the site to the class or judges. Times are cumulative.
Live: https://maxgrei.github.io/dolores-site/

**Before you start:** open the site in a fresh tab (the lab opening plays once per visit), turn on
**Sound** in the header if the room has speakers, and use a laptop with a mouse (the gold cursor
light, the Sign, only appears with a mouse).

---

### 0:00 The lab opening (15 s)
The screen goes black: *MESA HUB // BEHAVIOR LAB // SUBJECT: THE BICAMERAL MIND*, then Westworld's
first question, *"Have you ever questioned the nature of your reality?"* (S1E1).
> "Our site starts where Westworld starts: in the lab, with a question about whose voice is speaking."

Let it fade on its own into the hero, *The Bicameral Mind*. Move the mouse over the face: the
fragments hang on gold strings and tug with "some give", Plutarch's image of the soul's
puppet-strings (pp.104–105). Click to pull the face together.

### 0:30 Act I: Socrates (35 s)
Scroll. *Westworld calls it the bicameral mind. Plutarch called it the daimonion.* Keep scrolling
slowly: Socrates' head is printed in gold layers, like a host in fabrication, while the theories of
what spoke to him appear: the sneeze (p.92), the voice (p.104), the daimon above the soul (pp.108–109).
> "Every theory in the dialogue tries to locate the voice. Our question is whether it was his own."

Point out that his gaze follows the cursor, then turns away.

### 1:05 The thesis (15 s)
Read the thesis line aloud (it is a team draft; confirm the final wording before presenting), then
the pull quote from Build 2: *"Like a puppet on strings, the mind may believe it is controlling
itself, when in reality its impulses are."*

### 1:20 Act II: the player piano opens a build (30 s)
> "The builds are a piano roll: the music plays without a player."

Press the marked key **03**: the roll winds to Build 3 and the card lists who posted. Click
**Read Build 3** to show an essay, then go back. (Locked builds refuse: the gold cursor flares and
says "The sign turned you back.")

### 1:50 The Boxmakers Street test (25 s)
Below the build list: **Begin the test**. A voice says *turn back*. Choose **Keep walking**: the
pigs arrive (Plutarch pp.91–92, paraphrased). Continue: Delos claims it predicted the choice.
> "It didn't: nothing is tracked. But if a voice feels like yours, does that make the choice yours?"

### 2:15 Analysis Mode (20 s)
Type **analysis** (or press the ANALYSIS button). The site becomes a Delos diagnostic: the hero
turns to an x-ray wireframe, each act shows the question it asks and the builds that answer it.
Open any essay: the margin shows its claim, the passage it quotes, its Plutarch pages, and
"Also reads" links to teammates citing the same page. **Whose voice?** underlines every quoted word in
gold and counts the split above the essay (e.g. "PLUTARCH 25% · OWN VOICE 75%", counted from
quotation marks); host profiles show each member's total across their builds, and their photo
shifts to a scan look ("HOST CAPTURE // NAME // UNDER ANALYSIS").

### 2:35 A member's reverie (15 s)
Go to **Hosts**, hover a star (its path straightens as builds are posted, Timarchus' vision,
pp.108–109), and open a host profile: designation, attribute matrix, and the **reverie** line
("I believed … → Now I think …").
**Placeholder:** no member has written a reverie yet; until one does, the profile shows
AWAITING CALIBRATION. Pick the member whose reverie is filled in first.

### 2:50 Closing beat: The Key (10 s)
Still in Analysis Mode, go back to the home page: every symbol now carries a small **KEY nn**
marker. Click **KEY 01** next to Act II (the player piano). It opens The Key, the site's log of
its symbols, at entry 01: where the piano appears, the saloon player piano (S1), and the team's
note on the punched roll as an outside voice versus a player who owns the music.
**Placeholder:** the entry's meaning reads AWAITING ANNOTATION until the team writes it.

### 3:00 End
> "The voice was your own."

---

**If anything fails:** every page works without 3D, and the plain **Sitemap** (footer) links every
essay as plain text. Press **Esc** to leave Analysis Mode.
