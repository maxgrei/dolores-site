In Build 3, I argued that reconsolidation enables Epaminondas to consciously recognize and redirect impulses shaped by his past experiences. However, one might object that recognizing the influence of past experiences does not necessarily mean consciousness can change how someone responds to them. Epaminondas may understand why he practices restraint, but his decision to resist temptation could come from habits he developed long before making that decision. If so, consciousness might help him explain his behavior without actually causing him to behave differently. Epaminondas explains that unnecessary desires can “acquire force and strength as a result of long habit and education” (98). If someone’s past experiences can shape what they want, maybe those experiences can also shape how they respond to those desires. For example, Epaminondas might have been taught from a young age that accepting unnecessary wealth is undesirable and that resisting temptation is a sign of good character. If those lessons shaped his values, his decision to refuse money could reflect what he learned growing up rather than an independent conscious choice. The Timarchus myth strengthens this objection by illustrating how experiences can change the soul without a conscious decision. In Timarchus’s vision, the part of the soul connected to the body and its passions can be changed by pleasure and pain “into an irrational thing” (108). In other words, what someone experiences can shape how they behave without consciously deciding to change.

However, even if Epaminondas’s past experiences influenced his decision to practice restraint, they do not necessarily explain everything about how he develops self-control. While explaining why he refuses Theanor’s money, Epaminondas argues that someone who “voluntarily and repeatedly distances himself from perfectly just and permissible sources of profit” is “exercising and habituating himself to keep away from unjust or unlawful gain” (98-99). He recognizes that accepting money would be permissible, but believes refusing it will help him resist more serious temptations later; this demonstrates that Epaminondas understands why he practices restraint. Rather than treating his current discipline as something fixed, he sees how repeated practice could make him better at resisting temptation. Still, the fact that Epaminondas understands why he practices restraint does not necessarily mean that his understanding caused him to practice it. At the same time, his ability to recognize the effect of his present actions on his future character leaves open the possibility that consciousness influences how he develops.

Epaminondas’s description of the result of repeated restraint raises another question about consciousness’s role. He argues that someone who repeatedly rejects opportunities for profit will eventually “pursue the right without effort” (99). However, this goal raises another complication. If repeated restraint makes Epaminondas’s decisions effortless, he may eventually act without having to consciously decide whether to resist each time. In that case, repeatedly practicing restraint could develop into a habit that guides his future behavior. This challenges my earlier focus on consciousness redirecting impulses, since self-control may not always require conscious effort. Still, consciousness may play a role in developing those habits. By recognizing how repeated restraint could shape his future behavior, Epaminondas may consciously influence the habits that eventually allow him to resist temptation without effort.

In arguing this, I’ve modified my understanding of reconsolidation. In Build 3, I argued that consciousness enables Epaminondas to recognize and redirect impulses that he did not choose. However, I now think consciousness may influence his character less by controlling individual decisions and more by helping him develop habits that shape his future behavior. His past experiences may have influenced his choice to practice restraint in the first place, so readers cannot know for sure whether his consciousness caused that decision. Still, Epaminondas recognizes that repeatedly refusing permissible profit could eventually make resisting unlawful gain effortless. This suggests that reconsolidation might enable consciousness to influence the direction of his character development by connecting his present actions to the habits he wants to form, even if those habits eventually guide his behavior without conscious control.
